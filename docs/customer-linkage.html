Customer linkage

How a business sends an order to a customer, how the rider knows where to go, why a business can't reach customers who aren't theirs, and how one person never ends up with two accounts.

1The rule

Each person has one customer account, and they own it. A business never browses or searches customers. It only gets a link to a customer by sending that customer an order, and the link only shows what that business itself entered, plus the address of its own orders.

2How a business sends an order to a customer

  1. Ownerclicks New order and types what they got from the WhatsApp chat: the customer's email (required), name, phone, item, and the delivery address if they have it. There is no customer search box and no list of platform customers to pick from.
  2. Appchecks the email quietly on the server. It lowercases and trims it first, so Ngozi@Example.com and ngozi@example.com match.
    • Account exists → attach the order to it, and create this business's link if it's the first order.
    • No account → create an invited customer record for that email (no password yet), attach the order, and email an invite.
  3. Apptells the owner the same thing either way: “Order #1042 sent. Ngozi will be emailed.” The owner is never told whether the person already had an account. Otherwise anyone could type emails to find out who uses the platform.
  4. Customergets the email. With an account, they sign in and the order is there. Without one, they sign up with that same email and claim the invited record, so every order already sent to that email is waiting for them.
  5. Ownernow sees Ngozi in My customers, but only with what Adunni Boutique entered and Adunni Boutique's orders. Next time, the owner can pick Ngozi from this list instead of retyping.
Typing the email wrong is the main weak point. If the owner types ngozi@gmial.com, an invited record is made for an address nobody owns, and Ngozi never sees the code. Three safeguards: the form suggests corrections for common slips (“Did you mean gmail.com?”). The owner can fix the email until the order is packed. And the owner can still read the delivery code to the customer by phone as a last resort.

3Where the delivery address comes from

The address belongs to the order, not to the business's view of the customer. It comes from one of two places, and either way a copy is saved on that order.

Route A · the business enters it

The owner already has it from the chat: street, landmark, zone, map pin if the customer shared a location.

The customer sees it in the app and can correct it until the order is packed. Corrections go onto the order and the owner is notified.

Route B · the customer chooses it

The owner leaves the address blank. The order shows Awaiting address and the customer is asked: “Where should Adunni Boutique deliver?”

The customer picks one of their saved addresses or adds a new one. Only that one address is copied onto the order. The business never sees the customer's other saved addresses.

Who sees the delivery addressWhen
The business that sent the orderAlways, for its own orders
The rider on that orderFrom accepting until the delivery code is entered or the job is cancelled. Then it disappears from the rider's app
The customerAlways, plus all their saved addresses
Super adminZone only (for example “Lekki”), so issues can be grouped by area. Never the street or phone
Any other businessNever

If the customer moves, past orders keep the address they were delivered to. Editing a saved address only affects future orders.

4How double accounts are avoided

Email is the one identity key, because it's the only contact detail the app can verify without SMS. Phone numbers are stored, but they can't be used to match people. Riders and family members share phones, and an unverified number proves nothing.

One server function does the linking

send_order(email, name, phone, items, address?) runs with the business's identity. It looks the email up, creates the invited record or the link if needed, saves the order, and returns only the order number. It's the only way a business touches the customers table, so there's nothing to search or list.

Enforced in the database, not on screen

Row-level security on every table: a business reads business_customers and orders only where business_id is its own. Riders read jobs through a function that returns the address only while the job is active. Test it the way Verity does: log in as Gadget Hub, try to read Ngozi, expect nothing.

7Decide

Should the owner see “Ngozi already uses Ọ̀nà”? It would be convenient, but it tells one business something about a person's relationship with other businesses. I recommend not showing it. The owner gets the same message either way.
Route B adds a wait. If the owner leaves the address blank, nothing can be dispatched until the customer chooses one. Set a reminder (after 2 hours, prompt the owner to call), or require the owner to enter at least a zone and landmark.
A shared customer database across businesses is personal data held by a platform. For the study, use sample customers only (3.12 already says this). A real launch would need to check the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, which I haven't reviewed in detail.
