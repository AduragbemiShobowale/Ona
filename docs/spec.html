2Creating orders: one or many

One order

  1. Pick items from stock (quantity ≤ available). Stock is reserved.
  2. Customer's email (typo check). Address entered, or the customer chooses it.
  3. Delivery window from Ọ̀nà's slots: 8–10, 10–12, 12–2, 2–4, 4–6, 6–8 (Mon–Sat), 12–6 Sunday. Up to 15 orders per zone per slot; full slots are greyed out. Must be paid at least 1 hour before the slot starts, or it moves to the next slot.
  4. Vehicle suggested from item sizes; fee shown from Ọ̀nà's price list.

Many orders

  • Bulk entry: upload a spreadsheet (one row per order: email, items, quantities, window), as Verity does. Each row is checked against stock; bad rows are listed, good rows created.
  • Orders board: tick several packed orders → Request dispatch for 6 orders, with the total fee.
  • One transfer, one receipt covers them all. The super admin confirms them together.
  • One pickup visit: the rider collects every order on that route from the shop with a single pickup code; the bag count must match.

3Payment (Verity approach)

  1. Ownerrequests dispatch for one or more packed orders. Status Awaiting payment. Screen shows Ọ̀nà's account and a reference such as DSM-1042.
  2. Ownertransfers, enters the transfer reference, uploads the receipt (photo or PDF, private storage). Status Payment check. The 2-hour payment time limit pauses.
  3. Super adminchecks the bank app, then confirms → Ready for pickup, released to Operations. Or rejects with a reason (amount wrong, no transfer found, unreadable) → owner re-uploads. Third rejection closes the request.
  4. Appshows the super admin how long each receipt has waited. Target: confirmed within 15 minutes during operating hours (8am–8pm Mon–Sat, 12–6 Sun); alert at 15 minutes, escalated at 30. After-hours receipts are confirmed by 8:30am. Credits and waiting charges are applied to the next payment automatically.

4Dispatch and combined routes

  • Operations sees every business's paid orders in one queue, with window, drop zone, vehicle and bags.
  • The system suggests combining orders when pickups are within about 1 km, drops lie in the same direction, windows can all be met, and the bags fit one vehicle. Operations accepts, edits or keeps them separate.
  • Only available riders whose vehicle is big enough, who aren't paused and whose papers aren't expired are offered. The rider accepts the whole route.
  • Peak-traffic hours per zone are flagged on suggested routes before they are sent.

6On the road: congestion and rider changes

Congestion notice

The estimated arrival is updated from the rider's position. When it slips past the window end:

  • Customer: “Heavy traffic on the Lekki–Epe Expressway. New estimate 4:20pm.”
  • Business: the same, on the order.
  • The late delivery is recorded with the reason “traffic”, so the dashboard separates traffic delays from other causes (RO4, PM_F1).

Rider change mid-trip

  1. Rider reports a problem (breakdown, accident) or Operations decides to swap.
  2. Operations assigns a second rider with a fitting vehicle and sends them to the first rider's location.
  3. The first rider's app shows a handover code; the new rider types it after receiving the bags and photographing them.
  4. Customer and business: “Your rider has changed to Musa · Bike LAG 845 KJ because of a vehicle problem.” The customer's delivery code stays the same.

7Delivery, failure and override

  • Normal: customer checks the items, gives the delivery code, rider types it and photographs the handover.
  • Code locked (5 wrong tries) or customer's phone dead: rider calls Operations, who verify the customer by phone and record delivery with a reason. Logged in the audit trail; visible to the super admin and the business.
  • Customer not home or unreachable: failed attempt with reason; parcel goes back to the business. Re-delivery is a new dispatch with a new fee (customer-caused).
  • Customer refuses an item: failed attempt; complaint opened; parcel returned; the business decides about the goods.
  • Ọ̀nà-caused failure (wrong address taken, rider no-show at the door): free re-delivery.

15Starting the build: what “build on Verity” means

Verity is your existing app in your GitHub account (React + Supabase). It already does several things Ọ̀nà needs. Instead of starting from an empty project, you copy Verity into a new project for Ọ̀nà, keep what fits, change what's close, delete what doesn't apply, and add what's new. Verity itself is untouched, and the two apps never share a database.

Check before copying: if Verity was submitted for another course or built by a group, ask your supervisor whether reusing its code is allowed, and say in Chapter 4 that Ọ̀nà started from it. Re-using your own earlier work without saying so can be treated as self-plagiarism.
The alternative is to start from an empty project with the same tools. Nothing is lost except time: the pieces marked “Keep” would be written again.
